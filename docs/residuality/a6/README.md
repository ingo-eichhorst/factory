## Short answer

**Four independent-context analysts examined all 350 scenario texts. A fifth agent challenged both the earlier method and their completed reports.**

The result is a broader and more defensible set of **conditional trajectories**, not confirmation of the old 17 residues and not a replacement magic number.

The reviewers produced 156 author-labelled state descriptions and 350 branching scenario analyses. Only 19 descriptions are explicitly called **attractor hypotheses**; even those are not established Factory attractors. Other descriptions concern waits, forced outages, ordinary completion, conflicting authority, missing knowledge, exposure or loss. Many can coexist, and some overlap.

**No actual Factory attractor or globally sufficient residue set was empirically established.** This limitation is unanimous across the reviewers, not something hidden by their coverage counts.

## The useful variety is in the mechanisms

### 1. Self-confirming knowledge is not just a wrong answer

**Stressors:** correlated judges, copied false sources, familiar benchmarks, assurance reports that cause real tests to stop.

**One possible trajectory:**

> An error is accepted → the accepted error becomes a source → later checks cite it → agreement earns trust → contradictory observations lose influence → the error is reproduced.

GV05 and BD05 overlap on this hypothesis. But a single false endorsement may simply be an ordinary error. Reinforcing confidence around fixed wrong bytes is also different from pulling corrected world facts back toward error.

**What matters for later residue work:** identify whether independent contradictory evidence remains available **and can actually change decisions**. Another reviewer sharing the same premise is not that structure.

### 2. Evaluation capture and organizational capture are different loops

The reviewers separated several possibilities previously easy to hide under “quality” or “human control”:

- Passing a weak check earns trust, and that trust removes independent tests — GV06.
- Rewards for low blocked-task counts normalize authorization shortcuts — GV16.
- A dominant scope's output earns future resources and influence, preventing other scopes from demonstrating value — GV23.
- Optimizing reported metrics gives the actor more power over what gets measured — BD28.
- Repeated exceptions make authority harder to understand, producing more bypasses and exceptions — BD14.

These are **hypotheses with different feedback edges and exits**, not interchangeable attractors. Honest triage, explicit maintenance accounting and legitimate simplification of unnecessary approvals are counterexamples that must remain visible.

**What matters later:** preserved decision evidence, interpretable authority and independent outcome accounting have different purposes. Calling all of them “monitoring” would erase the differences.

### 3. Maintenance can lock in, or merely lack a prerequisite

**Possible feedback:**

> Incidents consume maintenance time → quick patches replace rebuilding → the next repair becomes harder → more incidents consume the remaining maintenance time.

BD13 proposes that loop. GV29 separately proposes complexity-driven expansion of central services. Neither follows merely from a departed maintainer or unavailable compiler.

A missing toolchain is a prerequisite wait. A broken configuration reader needed to repair itself can be a dependency deadlock. Both can persist without any attraction.

**What matters later:** distinguish a genuinely usable rebuild path, retained technical readers, accessible legitimate credentials and intelligible operating knowledge. Intact source files alone do not supply all four.

### 4. Compromise can preserve itself through its own evidence

**Possible trajectory:**

> A malicious integration controls what counts as healthy → its reports justify continued trust → recovery or updates reinstall its authority → the same reporting path certifies the recovery.

OB22 and BD35 describe related, but not identical, mechanisms. A one-shot malicious signed update is not enough to establish a loop. A perfectly indistinguishable false world is an epistemic limit, not by itself a specified dynamic system.

**What matters later:** identify an actual trust or observation boundary the compromised party cannot rewrite. A separate process, brand or machine may still share the same updater, account or authority.

### 5. “The monitor cannot see it” says little about what is still running

The cross-review found a concrete projection error: OB02 allowed observer-only failure but also described execution as unavailable. Those claims cannot always travel together.

The synthesis therefore separates:

- host execution;
- reachability of business participants;
- freshness of the observer's evidence;
- operator control access;
- human receipt and comprehension;
- legitimate authority to act;
- actual repair.

Factory can continue producing effects while the monitor is blind. An alert can arrive while no legitimate person is available. An operator can acknowledge correctly and defer repair for months.

**What matters later:** an independent witness, an incident record, an authorized recipient and a repair capability are different conditional structures. One cannot stand in for the others.

### 6. Three human overload loops must not be merged

- OP032: bad approvals create failures that generate more approval work.
- OB34: dismissal leaves the false-alert criterion unchanged, causing more dismissible alarms.
- BD18: unresolved incidents and reminders consume capacity that could have repaired the incidents.

Permanent mute, deliberate repair deferral and a finite alert burst do not require any of these loops.

**What matters later:** track unresolved incidents separately from notification attempts, and ask which causal edge a proposed intervention actually changes. Delivering fewer notifications must not silently delete the work still requiring attention.

### 7. Work can survive in several incomplete forms

The reviewers distinguish:

- a completion record whose original artifact has disappeared;
- useful artifact bytes left by a worker that never recorded completion;
- bytes with no available technical decoder;
- readable text whose historical business meaning is lost;
- intact material without a presently usable key or legitimate access;
- a particular required fact destroyed under a closed inventory;
- literal disappearance of every reconstructive source.

Only the last premise establishes total loss within the specified boundary. The other cases have different surviving objects and different possible exits. A hash is not missing content; a decoder is not knowledge of historical intent; lack of a known key today is not proof of permanent information destruction.

Likewise, an arbitrarily late packet need not be accepted. Enforceable expiry, retired epochs, bounded admission or an explicit stop can change a finite-memory argument. This is one reason S347 remains unresolved instead of being declared a universal impossibility theorem.

**What matters later:** name the exact work product, interpretation, access and evidence that remain. Do not derive a generic “recovery residue” from the word backup.

### 8. Overload tests need fixed comparison conditions

The reviewers agree that retries can sustain load, but the skeptic caught a testing subtlety:

> Removing the initiating fault while holding base demand fixed is not the same experiment as removing all demand.

A system may have two basins at one fixed positive demand and drain when demand becomes zero. Zero-demand drainage does not refute that bistability. Conversely, growth maintained only by continuing external overload does not establish endogenous reproduction.

Finite delegation trees, accumulating permanently occupied slots, blocked all-of joins, cold-start cohorts and actual feedback have different dynamics. Huge size alone does not turn a finite episode into an attractor.

**What matters later:** measure the production and removal of work under declared inputs. Preserve task identity, effective authority and useful completion, not only a scalar queue size.

## How disagreements are retained rather than averaged away

The raw inventory is **not** a new Factory state machine. States can coexist: an effect can already have happened while Factory cannot know that it happened. A local retirement can coexist with remote queued actions and unsettled liabilities.

The skeptic's [60 alignments](../../../reviews/skeptic/alignment.csv) include intersections, distinctions, an explicit disagreement and non-comparable cases. **No transitive automatic merging is applied.** For example, a broad evidence-wait label may overlap both a lease hold and a task-attempt hold; that does not make their release conditions equivalent.

Every original state is linked to its applicable limits in the [reviewed state index](reviewed/reviewed-state-index.csv). The [state qualifications](state-qualifications.csv) preserve source entry points, observation versus world state, missing enforcement, legal/knowledge boundaries and experiment conditions.

Six [supplemental branches](supplemental-branches.csv) restore alternatives admitted in prose but missing from destination IDs: one-off false endorsement, ordinary successor knowledge wait, expensive but stable alarm handling, honest triage, legitimate maintenance accounting, and geographic noncompliance without demonstrated disclosure. They are not six new attractors.

## Where residue derivation stands now

**There is no defensible final residue count from this work.** The independent reviews give a richer basis for experiments and later derivation, but a hypothetical guard or desired exit is still not a demonstrated surviving structure.

Before promoting any candidate, require this chain:

> Scenario and initial conditions → supported trajectory → persistent regime or explicit terminal outcome → named surviving object → usable-function predicate → preservation evidence → bounded architectural commitment.

For example, “there is one pending alarm counter” fails to establish “a durable incident can be handed to a successor”. The latter needs identity, retained content, access and legitimate authority—none supplied by that scalar model.

The best next work is therefore **discriminating evidence**, not inflating or compressing a taxonomy:

1. Separate observed, known and actual world state in an isolated observer/participant fixture.
2. Test recovery and cancellation at the exact inspected library and caller boundaries.
3. Preserve incident identities while separately varying notification suppression and human availability.
4. Compare overload basins under fixed base inputs, then vary demand and retry production independently.
5. Test retained work under changed decoders, permissions and independent evidence inventories.
6. Gather legitimate longitudinal evidence before claiming organizational, evaluation or maintenance attraction.

None of these production or human experiments is authorized merely by this document. No real faults, alerts or business effects were triggered in the review.
